Why reading silently one more time doesn't work

By the tenth read-through, your eyes aren't reading your manuscript anymore — they're reading your memory of it. That's why the same typo survives five proofreading passes and gets caught by the first reader who leaves a review. Hearing your writing, rather than re-reading it, engages a different part of your attention, which is why professional audiobook production catches errors publishers' own editors missed.

A workflow that actually catches things

1. Upload the actual file you're about to publish

Not your working draft — the finished .epub or .docx file, exactly as it'll go to Google Play Books, KDP, Apple Books, or Kobo. Formatting conversion introduces its own errors your draft never had.

2. Let automatic detection do a first pass

Before you even press play, Read Aloud scans for mechanical issues — repeated words, double spaces, missing punctuation spacing — and flags them automatically, along with a readability score for the whole book.

3. Listen chapter by chapter, flag as you go

Press play and follow the paragraph highlighting. Hit the flag button (or press F) the moment something sounds wrong — don't stop to fix it, just mark it and keep moving. Fixing breaks your listening flow and you'll miss the next error.

4. Export your flagged list and fix everything in one sitting

Download the full list of flagged lines as a text file and work through it as a checklist, rather than fixing piecemeal while you listen.
