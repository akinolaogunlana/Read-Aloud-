A Free Alternative to Speechify and NaturalReader

To be fair to both — what the paid tools actually do better

Speechify and NaturalReader are real products solving a broader problem than this tool does. Their paid tiers include premium AI voices (often licensed from ElevenLabs-quality providers) that sound noticeably more natural than a browser's built-in voice. They offer native mobile apps with offline playback, OCR to scan physical books and documents, browser extensions that work across any website, and voice cloning in some tiers. If you want a general-purpose audiobook-style reader for consuming books, articles, and PDFs across every device you own, they're built for exactly that, and the subscription buys real capability.

What this tool is actually built for instead

Read Aloud isn't trying to be a smaller version of those products — it's built for a narrower job: proofreading your own writing before you publish or submit it. That difference shows up in what's actually here: paragraph-level highlighting synced to playback, one-click flagging of problem lines with a full exportable checklist, an automatic readability score, and automatic detection of repeated words and spacing errors — none of which are the focus of a general-purpose reading app.

The practical trade-offs, stated plainly

  • Voice quality: Your browser's built-in voice, not a licensed premium AI voice. Noticeably more robotic than Speechify's paid tiers.
  • Cost: $0, permanently, versus $79–159/year.
  • Account: None needed here — no signup, no login, nothing to cancel later.
  • Where processing happens: Entirely in your browser for file uploads — nothing sent to a server. (The article-by-URL feature is the one exception; see the Privacy Policy.)
  • Platform: Any browser, any device — no app to install, but also no offline mode.

Who this is actually for

If you're choosing based on voice quality for long-form listening, or want offline mobile playback, a paid tool is the better fit. If what you need is a focused, free way to hear your own manuscript, essay, or report before publishing — with tools built specifically for catching your own errors, not just for listening — this is built for that instead.
