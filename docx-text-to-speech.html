Turn Your Word Document Into Something You Can Listen To

Upload a .docx file and Read Aloud converts its real structure — headings, paragraphs — into a listening review session, with each paragraph highlighted as it's spoken.

Upload a .docx file →

Why DOCX works especially well here

Word documents carry real structure — actual headings, actual paragraph breaks — unlike a PDF, which is mostly just positioned text with no memory of what a "paragraph" even is. That structure is what lets Read Aloud highlight your document at the paragraph level, not just the page level, so you can follow along precisely as it's read back to you.

Who uses this

Writers doing a final read-through before sending a draft to an editor or client. Students proofing an essay or thesis chapter written in Word. Authors drafting a manuscript before converting it to EPUB for publishing. Anyone who's stared at the same paragraph in Word so many times their eyes stop actually seeing it.

What happens to your file

Nothing leaves your browser. The .docx is parsed locally using an open-source library (Mammoth.js) that runs entirely client-side — there's no upload, no server copy, no account needed.

What you get beyond just listening

Flag any line that needs a fix as you hear it, adjustable reading speed, a reading-level score for the whole document, and automatic detection of mechanical issues like repeated words or double spaces — all before you even press play, if you want.
