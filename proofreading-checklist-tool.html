Stop Losing Track of What You Meant to Fix

Fixing an error the moment you hear it breaks your listening flow, and you miss the next one. Flag it instead, keep listening, and fix everything afterward from one exported checklist.

Start flagging as you listen →

The problem with fixing errors as you go

The instinct, the moment you hear an awkward sentence, is to stop and fix it right away. That instinct is exactly what makes a listening proofread less effective than it could be — stopping to edit breaks your attention, and by the time you've fixed one sentence, you're no longer listening closely enough to catch the next one. A better pattern, used by professional audiobook proofers, is to mark and keep moving, then fix everything in a single dedicated editing pass afterward.

How it works here

While listening, press the flag icon (or hit F on your keyboard) the instant something sounds off — a typo, an awkward phrase, a fact that contradicts something earlier. No need to stop, rewind, or even pause. Keep listening. Every flagged line gets a visible marker in the text and a running count in the sidebar.

Automatic flags join the list too

Mechanical issues — repeated words, double spaces, oddly long paragraphs — get auto-detected and added to the same list before you even start listening, tagged separately so you can tell them apart from what you flagged yourself.

Export when you're done

One click downloads every flagged line as a plain text file, grouped by section, with the reason noted for anything auto-detected. Work through it as a checklist in your word processor, instead of trying to remember which of forty chapters had the problem you half-noticed three hours ago.
