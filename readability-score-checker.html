What the score actually measures

Flesch Reading Ease scores a text from 0 to 100 based on average sentence length and average syllables per word — higher means easier to read. The related Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level translates that same math into a U.S. school grade level, so a score of "8.2" means the text is roughly as complex as typical 8th-grade reading material. Both formulas date back decades and are still the standard most style guides, plain-language laws, and content teams actually use.

Why this matters beyond just curiosity

A novel aimed at general adult readers usually lands comfortably in the "standard" range. Academic or technical writing often scores much harder — sometimes intentionally, sometimes as a sign that sentences have gotten too long or too dense without anyone noticing. If your target reader is a broad, general audience and your score comes back "dense/academic," that's worth a second look before you publish.

What you get, automatically

Upload any document — manuscript, article, report, thesis chapter — and the score, grade level, total word count, and an estimated read-aloud time are calculated instantly and shown in the sidebar the moment your file loads. No separate step, no extra upload.

How it's calculated

Entirely in your browser, using the standard Flesch formulas and a syllable-counting heuristic — no file or text is ever sent anywhere to compute this.
